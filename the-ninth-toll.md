# The Ninth Toll

**An original campaign for levels 1–7 · ~12–16 sessions · sandbox pointcrawl**

Compatible with fifth edition rules. Uses only SRD 5.1 / 5.2.1 creatures and mechanics (CC-BY-4.0, Wizards of the Coast). All setting, characters, and adventure content herein are original.

---

## Premise

The valley of **Emberlow** lies under a cliff face, and on that cliff stands the **Chantry of Hours** — a bell tower built during the Grey Year, seventy-odd years ago, when plague came up the river road.

The bell was meant as a mercy. It takes grief. It also takes everything attached to the grief.

When the bell tolls, everyone in the valley forgets something: a person, a promise, a place they used to go. They wake with an extra chair at the table and no idea who sat in it. The valley has adapted rather than resisted — everyone keeps ledgers, everyone tattoos the names of their children on their forearms, and nobody goes up the cliff road.

Eight tolls have sounded. The party arrives in the interval before the ninth.

**Why the party is immune:** the bell reaches only what is written in the town Registry. The party are strangers, unregistered — for now. (See *The Registry Clock*, below. This is the campaign's signature mechanic.)

---

## The Antagonist

**Wren Ashvault, the Hourwarden.** Roughly seventy. Not a monster; a woman who cannot stop.

In the Grey Year she ordered the valley gates sealed against the sick — including her own brother, who died on the wrong side of them. She rang the first toll to unmake her own guilt, and it worked. Every toll since has been her failing to stop, each one covering the last, an addiction with a bell rope.

She does not want to be worshipped or obeyed. She wants to be stopped and cannot arrange it herself, because the tower has learned to defend her — and because each toll takes a little more of the woman who would have wanted stopping.

**Her plan, unopposed:** ring the ninth toll, which is large enough to take the valley's memory of *itself*. Emberlow would not be destroyed. It would simply stop being anywhere, and no one outside would recall that it had been.

## The Reliquary Draw

*The randomizing engine — the campaign's shape differs every run.*

In Session 1 the party recovers a water-stained box of keepsakes from a drowned cart: a brass locket, a child's shoe, a bone fishhook, a wedding knife, a folded map, a dog's collar, a soldier's chit, an empty inkwell, a lock of hair. **Draw four.**

- **Draws 1–3 determine the three Anchors.** Each keepsake maps to a location and to a person the valley has forgotten. Recovering an Anchor restores that memory to everyone in the valley at once — a public, uncontrollable event with consequences the party cannot predict.
- **Draw 4 determines the Ally** — the one resident who has resisted the bell, and is therefore considered mad. Could be the archivist, the quarry revenant, the hag's servant, a child, the gravedigger.
- **The remaining keepsakes are red herrings** that still mean something to someone.

Keep the mapping table server-side. The DM (human or AI) doesn't know the campaign's shape until the draw.

## The Registry Clock

The party's immunity is temporary and *self-inflicted*.

Every time the party formally enters the valley's civic life — sign an inn ledger, accept a bounty, get named in a will, take a title, marry, get arrested, get thanked publicly — they gain a **Registry mark**. Track per character.

- **0 marks:** immune to the bell.
- **1–2 marks:** on a toll, lose one minor memory. Player chooses from a list; DM notes it in the record.
- **3+ marks:** on a toll, roll on the Forgetting table. May lose a proficiency, an NPC relationship, a spell, or a plot fact the character knew.

This is the campaign's central tension and its best mechanic: **acting effectively in Emberlow requires becoming part of Emberlow, which makes you vulnerable to the thing you came to stop.** Players who stay ghosts stay safe and accomplish nothing.

## The Toll Clock

Nine stages. The bell advances on a timetable *and* in reaction to party pressure. Ringing is not silent — the whole valley hears it and everyone reacts.

| Toll | Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1–8 | Historical | Backstory; visible as gaps in the record |
| **9** | ~Session 12, or immediately if Wren is directly threatened before the Anchors are secured | Takes the valley's memory of itself |

**Advancing the clock:** each Anchor recovered pushes Wren toward the ninth toll (she feels the restorations as pressure). Each faction turned against her delays it. The party effectively chooses their own pacing — and the fastest route to the truth is also the fastest route to the ending.

## Locations (pointcrawl — visit in any order)

**1. Emberlow Town** — Valley floor. Home base. Everything is written down twice. The Ledgerhands' archive is the closest thing to institutional memory and it has holes cut in it. *Encounters: cult fanatics, bandit captain, doppelganger.*

**2. The Chantry of Hours** — The tower. Reachable from Session 2 and will kill a low-level party. Vertical dungeon, 6 levels, the mechanism at the top. *Encounters: animated armor, wraith, specters, invisible stalker.*

**3. The Sundered Orchard** — A **green hag** grows fruit that tastes like other people's memories. She is the valley's black market in remembering and she is *not* aligned with Wren — competitors, not allies. Her prices are the campaign's best moral trap.

**4. The Weir** — Fishing hamlet where the river runs backward for one hour after dawn. Something in the water has been eating what the bell throws away. *Encounters: water weirds, ghasts, will-o'-wisps.*

**5. The Cairnworks** — Quarry and necropolis. The quarry-folk carve memories into stone so the bell can't take them. A **revenant** here remembers *everything*, including all nine of the Grey Year's atrocities, and has been driven to ruin by it. Strong Ally candidate; strong villain-mirror.

**6. Ashvault Manor** — Wren's family seat. The staff still serve a household that no longer exists, setting the table for names nobody can read. *Encounters: mimic, ghouls, shadows.*

**7. The Drowned Chapel** — Submerged pre-tower shrine. Holds the bell's original design and the fact that it was never meant to be rung more than once. *Encounters: swarm of rats, giant spiders, a banshee.*

**8. The Silt Road Caravanserai** — Outside the valley. Traders who never come twice, because they don't remember coming once. Information hub, resupply, and the only place to learn what the outside world *doesn't* know about Emberlow.

|---|---|---|
| **The Ledgerhands** | The tolls documented, not stopped | Archive access; bureaucratic obstruction of Wren |
| **The Quiet** | More tolls, sooner — forgetting as grace | Cult muscle; but they'll sabotage every Anchor |
| **The Cairn-kin** | Nothing forgotten, ever, at any cost | Stone-carved evidence; zealotry that may burn the town |
| **The Hourwarden** | To be stopped, and to not be stopped | — |

## Endgame — three resolutions

1. **Silence the bell.** The valley keeps its holes. Everyone lost stays lost. Stable, and quietly tragic.
2. **Restore everything.** Ring the anchors in reverse. Emberlow gets seventy years of grief back in one night, including the truth about the gates — and will likely tear itself apart. Honest, and possibly catastrophic.
3. **Ring the ninth.** Grant the mercy. The valley forgets itself and becomes peaceful, empty, unfindable. The party walks out and cannot afterwards say where they've been.

No option is coded as correct. All three should be reachable, and the party should have to argue about it.

## Notes for the engine

The reason this campaign is shaped the way it is: **every piece of world state here is enumerable.**

```
draw:            [anchor_1, anchor_2, anchor_3, ally_id]
toll_count:      int (8 at start)
anchors:         {id: hidden | located | recovered}
registry_marks:  {character_id: int}
memories_lost:   {character_id: [fact_id]}
locations:       {id: unvisited | visited | resolved}
factions:        {id: hostile | neutral | allied}
wren_state:      {knows_party: bool, pressure: int, cornered: bool}
truths_known:    [fact_id]      # per character, not per party
```

That last line matters for the stand-in AI. `truths_known` is **per character**, so an absent player's proxy acts on what *their* character learned — not on the campaign record. It's also the reason this premise suits the app: a campaign about memory, absence, and incomplete records gives an AI proxy's imperfect knowledge a diegetic explanation. When the stand-in plays someone slightly wrong, the fiction already has a word for that.
